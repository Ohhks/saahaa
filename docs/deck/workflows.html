Both sides05
Workflow three

The shop, from a shelf to a delivery

Opening the shop

  • Tap items from a ready-made list. ~180 real Hyderabad SKUs with real prices — about six seconds each, no typing.
  • Add a photo, a name, a price. Anyone can list what they actually sell.
  • MRP is enforced. The app refuses to list above it, so a shop cannot quietly overcharge.
  • Open or closed is one switch. Closed means no orders can reach you — not a queue of angry customers.

Taking an order

  • Accept, pack, weigh. The screen shows what the customer paid and what you keep.
  • Something out of stock? Offer a similar item, refund that line, or ask her — she has 90 seconds, then it refunds automatically.
  • Delivery or pickup. Pickup shows the customer's code at the counter.
  • 3% from your side, capped at ₹25 — and your first 30 orders are free of it entirely.

The money · at scale08
Every way to earn

What the app can do for the people who work

A professional

  • Keep the whole quote. Roughly ₹264 more per ₹1,000 job than a commission app — about ₹5,300 a month on twenty jobs.
  • Get found without advertising. A public page, built and kept current from your work. Share the link on WhatsApp — it is your professional card.
  • Win work by being trusted, not cheapest. Sealed rates are scored on trust and distance as much as price.
  • Climb and pay less. SAAHAA Certified drops the customer's charge from 8% to 6%, which makes you the better-priced pro without cutting your own rate.
  • Be paid on the day. Money released to your UPI when the customer confirms — no thirty-day terms.
  • Repeat customers, on the record. "Book again" puts you one tap away for anyone you have worked for.

A shop

  • Open in an evening. Tap items from a ready list; a storefront is live the same day.
  • First 30 orders free. Then 3%, capped at ₹25 — a ₹2,000 basket costs ₹25, not ₹60.
  • Delivery without hiring anybody. A visible per-distance fee, ₹5 of which is dispatch and the rest the rider's.
  • Sell what the aggregators will not list. Your own products, your own photos, your own prices under MRP.
  • No dark-store competing with you. The platform does not hold stock, so it is never your rival.

Both

  • One number, two accounts. Trade as a pro and buy as a customer — separate wallets, separate histories, one phone.
Earning09
Every way to save

What the app can do for the people who buy

On a service

  • 21–29% less than the same job on a commission app, because there is no quarter to fund.
  • Ask several pros for a price without losing the one you have — your booking stays held while you ask, so asking costs nothing.
  • The price is locked before you book and cannot rise. Extra work needs your say-so.
  • A warranty on the work, where the category carries one.
  • Full refund if you cancel before the pro sets out.

On a shop order

  • Never above MRP. The app refuses to list an item above it.
  • Delivery priced by distance, shown as its own line — ₹19 up to 2 km, not a mystery fee.
  • Out of stock is your decision, not theirs: a similar item, a refund, or ask me.
  • Money held until you confirm, on a grocery order as much as a plumber.
  • Buy from the shop on your street, so the margin stays in the neighbourhood.

A household booking two services a month and ordering weekly saves in the region of ₹700–900 a month against commission apps — before counting what stays in the neighbourhood rather than leaving it.

What this is not, yet

The limits, stated before anyone asks

  • A neighbourhood app, by design. Everything lives on one device today. Measured: one pincode is comfortable, a ward fits, a suburb needs a desktop. Past that the answer is the server, not a bigger phone.
  • Payments run in sandbox until the gateway is switched on, and every screen that moves money says so.
  • No SMS, deliberately. Removed from the plan, not deferred — which is why SAAHAA never sends you a code, and anyone claiming otherwise is not us.
  • Client-side auth is UI, not authorization. Written down in the security document rather than glossed over. Real money needs the server-side rails, and those are built and waiting.
  • A device that fills up says so. Loudly, at the top of every screen, and it cannot be dismissed — because silently failing to save would be worse than crashing.
  • One owner, one city. Ten pros, three shops, fifty households, then the next pincode.

Stating these is the point. A marketplace asking people to trust it with their livelihood does not get to be vague about what it cannot do yet.
