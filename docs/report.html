Build statement · v6.1.0 “Open Circle”

Saahaa

1 Sept 2026 · 26 modules · 24 categories · 87 tests green

SAAHAA now runs entirely on GitHub + Supabase free tier, and most of it runs without you. Money is locked and released by the database, not the browser; multiple pros can bid on one job without racing each other to the bottom; and the whole thing is designed around one constraint that matters more than any feature — one person operates this in about fifteen minutes a day. Two rounds of a five-agent voting panel produced the decisions below; where a vote overturned something already shipped, that is recorded with the reasoning.

How the decisions were made

Five agents, one ballot each, scored by confidence. Three votes changed the product materially — two of them corrected things that were already shipped and wrong.

Marketplace flow conf 5

Auto-suggest one pro who has already accepted, plus “see others”. Not auto-assign (breaks humans-decide), not a list (two extra taps).

Biggest risk in a 3-tap flow is the confirm-moment trust collapse: you deleted every step that normally builds confidence, so all the doubt lands on one screen. The Locked-Match card is the fix.

Local commerce changed the economics

Retail cannot carry the 10% service rate. A kirana's gross margin on staples is 3–6%, so a 10% cut is larger than the entire margin on the items people order most. The shop would refuse, or quietly inflate MRP and destroy price trust.

Adopted: 3% staples / 5% higher-margin, capped per order, charged to the shop. Delivery is a separate visible line to the customer.

Platform architecture conf 5

Native ES modules for real boundaries, plus a single-file bundle so double-clicking still works. One store, versioned migrations with rollback, runtime registries as the open/closed seam, flags as the kill-switch, an in-app test suite as CI.

Trust & payments changed the tax model

The “8% profit + 2% GST” label was wrong. GST on a marketplace commission is 18% of the commission, not 2% of the deal. Same customer total, correct invoice.

Also adopted: tiered escrow rather than review-everything, and the rule that no photo means never auto-release.

UI / UX conf 5

Midnight plum + gold, flat with soft depth. The logo is already an ornate artefact — the UI must be the quiet frame around it, not compete with it.

Light content canvas, plum brand chrome: your audience is on sunlit ₹8–15k Android LCDs where a full dark theme turns to grey mud.

Where the panel split

Only one real disagreement: the customer-savings claim. One voter showed near-parity at a ₹1,000 job; the honest framing is equal take-home — a pro paid the same earns 33% more here, so they can quote less.

The app now prints that assumption on screen under every savings figure rather than claiming a number it can't defend.

Every rupee in the app is an integer number of paise. Floats never touch a currency value, and the test suite asserts that the parts always sum back to the whole.

Shops · 8 · every one lists its own products

One shop per cart

Enforced, with a friendly swap dialog. Multi-shop carts mean split delivery, split settlement, split blame — unreasonable to run and unpleasant to debug.

You choose the substitution rule

Per line: similar brand OK, ask me, or just refund. Defaults per category — staples swap, pharmacy and meat never. No reply in 90 seconds refunds the line. Nothing is ever swapped silently.

“Est.” until it's weighed

Anything sold by weight shows an estimate and bills on the actual weighed weight. Hiding that is the single biggest trust bug in Indian grocery apps.

How an order moves

Two machines, because a plumber's appointment and a bag of tomatoes are genuinely different objects. Illegal transitions throw loudly rather than passing silently — the suite checks that you cannot skip the arrival code.

Service · from three taps to settled

MATCHING→ASSIGNED→EN_ROUTE→ ARRIVED · OTP→IN_PROGRESS→ WORK_DONE · photo→SETTLED→RATED

The customer reads out a 4-digit code at the door — that proves the right person arrived, which is the real safety risk, not the payment. At the end the pro attaches a photo and the customer confirms with one tap; no second code to type, because a second code is a second chance for someone to be unreachable while the pro's money is stranded. No photo means it never auto-releases — that one rule does more anti-fraud work than everything else combined.

Grocery · from cart to settled

R_PLACED→R_ACCEPTED→R_PICKING→ R_SUB_PENDING→R_PACKED · weighed→ R_OUT→R_DELIVERED · OTP→R_SETTLED

Reviewing every job by hand stops scaling at about 30 bookings a day. Releasing everything automatically hands fraudsters an ATM. The risk score picks which one you get.

A dispute takes three taps to raise, one to settle

Report → pick a reason chip → submit. Escrow freezes on the spot. If the pro never arrived and no start code was entered, it refunds in full with no admin involvement at all.

Shipping updates without breaking things

This was the actual brief. Here is what now protects you, and the failure it was built for.

What could breakWhat stops it
Users lose their data on an updateVersioned migration chain writes a snapshot first, migrates, verifies, and rolls back automatically if anything throws. Your v5 accounts, orders and chats all survived this rewrite.
Two tabs migrate at once and corrupt the treeA migration lock with a 10-second TTL; every step is idempotent, and the suite asserts that migrating twice equals migrating once.
A new feature breaks an old oneFeatures ship behind a flag defaulting to off. Turn it on for yourself for one release, default it on in the next, delete the flag the release after.
Money quietly driftsInteger paise, a reconciles assertion on every quote, a build lint that rejects float arithmetic outside money.js, and an admin screen showing escrow against ledger replay side by side.
You rename a category and old orders render blankRegistry entries are append-only. An unknown id returns a tombstone that renders “Legacy: x” instead of throwing, and migrations remap ids rather than dropping them.
One bad screen white-screens the appEach view renders inside try/catch and fails to an error card with the version on it. Everything else still works.
A stale cached stylesheet meets new codeThe build stamps every ?v= from BUILD_ID. This bit us once during the build and is now automated so it can't recur.
You can't get back to a working versionEvery release is git-tagged and every migration leaves a restorable snapshot listed in the admin panel. An untagged release isn't a release.

If you find yourself editing orders.js or app.js to add a feature, that is the signal to stop: the registry contract is missing a field. Extend the contract first, as its own commit, with tests.

The admin console, inside the site

Sign in as admin on the normal login screen, or open #/admin. Eight sections — a console with twenty nav items is an unused console. Each screen separates what you decide from what the system computes.

You decide

  • Approve or reject partners, and their tier upgrades
  • Approve, reject or spot-check listings
  • Release 100%, release 60%, or refund a dispute — with a written reason
  • Suspend or unsuspend a partner; ban with hash propagation
  • Hide a review; action a flagged chat
  • Mark a payout batch paid, against its UTR
  • Flip feature flags; change the admin password; restore a backup

The system computes

  • Users, partners, shops, orders, revenue, GMV, escrow held, customer savings
  • Booking statistics by stage and rating distribution
  • Trust scores, verification bands, partner earnings
  • Escrow tiering and release timers
  • Dispute SLA clock, auto-resolution, evidence bundling
  • Off-platform payment detection in chat
  • GST liability at 18% of the fee, and the payout queue
  • Health checks, registry integrity, storage quota, the audit log

The screen that matters most balanced

Reconciliation sits at the top of Finance and shows three numbers that must agree: escrow held, ledger replay, and the difference. If they ever disagree, every other number in the console is fiction — which is exactly why it sits above them rather than buried in a tab.

Running on GitHub + Supabase, for ₹0

The whole system now has a real backend on a free tier. The rule the schema enforces is one sentence long, and everything else follows from it.

The browser cannot move a rupee

The anon key ships inside a public page, so anything the browser is allowed to write is effectively a public API. Every money table therefore has INSERT, UPDATE and DELETE revoked and no write policy at all. The only things that can move money are database functions that re-check the caller, the state machine and the arithmetic in SQL.

How the money is verified — the virtual OTP

There is no paid SMS, and Indian DLT registration is a blocker anyway. So the verification primitive is a code that exists only inside the app. What makes it trustworthy is the direction it travels, and the fact that it alternates.

Check-in customer → pro

The customer's screen shows the code; the pro types it. That proves the pro is physically at the door and the customer agreed to start.

Check-out pro → customer

Now it reverses: the pro's screen shows the code; the customer types it. That proves the customer accepted the finished work. Running the same direction twice would mean one screenshot at the start covers both gates.

Stored asa plain hash of a 6-digit code is brute-forced in milliseconds, so the pepper is mandatorysalted + peppered SHA-256

Both sides have something at stake

The customer's money is locked at booking. The pro's stake is a holdback: 10% of each payout, capped at ₹500, released after seven clean days. They never pay to join — a cold-start marketplace cannot demand cash up front from supply — but their skin in the game grows exactly as their volume, and therefore their capacity to defraud, grows.

When several pros want the same job

You asked for bidding, done reasonably. Reasonably is the hard part, so the panel made it mechanical rather than aspirational. Four rules, interlocking.

1 · The floor stops the cliff

No bid below 0.85× the fair price, never under ₹199. So the worst possible SAAHAA outcome still beats what a 25%-commission app typically pays. The promise becomes a constraint instead of a slogan.

2 · The peaked score stops the slide

This is the one that actually works. The price score is highest at the fair price, not at the floor — so undercutting costs points and wins nothing. A floor on its own just becomes the new market price.

3 · The ceiling caps collusion

A ring cannot extract more than 1.30×, and the fixed-price fallback is the credible threat against one. Bids are sealed — nobody sees another's price, so a ring cannot even verify its own members are complying.

4 · Honest bidders cannot be haggled

The customer's one-tap counter-offer is unavailable against anyone who bid at or below the fair price. Bidding honestly is strictly safer than bidding opportunistically. One counter per request, ever — no haggling loop.

A worked auction · plumbing, fair price ₹500

Bidder A · bids the floor, 9 km away, brand new, bid at 13 min₹450 → score 47
Bidder B · bids fair price, 2.5 km, 4.8★ over 120 jobs, bid at 1 min₹500 → score 83
ResultB wins by 36

A is fifty rupees cheaper and loses decisively. They still appear under a Cheapest tab, labelled honestly — hiding the cheap bid entirely reads as a con — but the default recommendation steers to the fair-price bidder. After the auction closes, A is told exactly why: "you were ₹50 cheaper and still lost; it came down to distance and reviews, not price." That delayed feedback is the teaching loop. Showing live prices during the window would teach the opposite lesson.

Five places where bidding is banned outright

Banned forBecause

Bidding never taxes the fast path. Instant Book stays three taps and stays the default; bidding is a grey link underneath it, and becomes the primary path only when there is no pro nearby, or the job is large, or the price is genuinely unknowable.

What runs itself, and what waits for you

You asked me to vote on this too. Here is where I drew the line, and why. The scarcest resource in this system is your attention — not compute, not money.

Fully automatic ~60% of tasks

  • Escrow locks, and releases — target 85% with no human
  • Auctions open, close, expire and fall back
  • No-shows detect and refund themselves
  • ~60% of disputes resolve with nobody watching
  • Holdbacks mature; trust scores recompute
  • Nightly reconciliation, backups, keepalive
  • Tests, build, deploy, smoke test

You only ~7% — and all the risk

  • A partner's first in-home job. One bad actor in a home ends the business, and no free signal predicts it.
  • Any safety, harassment or injury report. Needs a human in minutes. An automated "we've refunded you" reply to an assault report is catastrophic — which is why it is a separate table, not a dispute.
  • Permanent bans. Irreversible action against someone's livelihood needs a name attached.
  • Fee changes. Silently rewrites every open order at once.
  • Applying a database migration. The free tier has no point-in-time recovery, so no automation may ever touch production data.

Your 15 minutes

One screen — the TODAY queue — showing only what is blocked on a human, ordered by money at risk. If it is empty, the app says so and shows you nothing else. That view exists because an eight-section admin console is a reporting tool; at 50 orders a day it turns the daily ritual into 45 minutes, and a 45-minute daily ritual gets skipped inside three weeks.

Turn most of this OFF for the first 30 days

With zero users, automation has nothing to work on and manual is genuinely better. Switch off auto-release (look at every settlement — it is how you learn where the rules are wrong), auto-disputes (your first twenty are the curriculum), algorithmic matching (you will know all ten partners personally), and bidding itself — it needs six or more competing pros or it produces empty auctions that just look broken.

Keep on from day one: the ledger, escrow holding, the state machines, the OTP, backups and the tests. The plumbing, not the intelligence.

Where it stands

Tests 87 / 87

Money reconciliation, ledger invariants, transition legality, migration idempotence, referential integrity, catalog validity, trust bounds, and the bidding band.

They now run in CI under plain Node with honest stubs — real WebCrypto, and document as a poison pill so a browser-only suite identifies itself mechanically rather than by anyone's judgement.

Bugs the new tests caught

  • Quote-based categories were being given a percentage price band — null ?? DEFAULT fell through, when null was exactly the “quote only” signal
  • The band inverted for cheap categories: a ₹180 ironing job's travel minimum exceeded its own fair price, putting the floor above the ceiling
  • A ledger fixture that looked right but left a customer with a negative balance — the invariant was correct to reject it

Earlier rounds also fixed: re-seeding on reload, sheets outliving their screen, a stale cached stylesheet, and the emblem scattering.

What is honestly not safe yet
