The theory

Three commitments, and everything else follows

Money you can audit

Every rupee is an entry in a double-entry, hash-chained, append-only ledger. Balances are not stored — they are derived by replaying the chain. A claim can never post a leg, because that would be minting money by typing.

One code per person

A customer is C20262001 forever. The same code signs her in and is the code she reads out at the door. No OTP, no SMS bill, nothing to intercept — and nothing to forget on a phone with no signal.

Prices that add up

A column of figures must sum to the total printed beside it. If it cannot in whole rupees, the whole column goes to paise. The amount she types into her bank app is a whole rupee, rounded up, and the rounding is ours.

The ₹40 is deliberately smaller than the ₹100 minimum stake: cancelling early must never cost a pro as much as simply not turning up.

The pro

He keeps his quote — and climbs a ladder he can see

A pro sets his own rate. SAAHAA never discounts him, never runs a sale on his labour, and never takes a percentage of it. What he is quoted is what he is paid, on every job, at every tier. The only money that ever leaves his side is a ₹40 fee if he cancels.

Tier 4 is the one lever we pull on price, and it pulls in his favour: a certified pro's customer is charged 6% instead of 8%, which makes him cheaper to hire without making him cheaper.

What he puts in

A stake of ₹100, or 5% of the job, capped at ₹500 — locked while he holds the job and returned when it closes. It is the reason a slot booked is a slot worked.

What he waits for

A 10% holdback, released after 7 clean days. Not a fee — his own money, held briefly, so a customer with a complaint on day three still has something to be made whole from.

What he takes out

Withdrawals from ₹10. Payouts are settled the next morning, against the bank statement — never against a promise on a screen.

What he shares

Every earner gets a QR code of his own page. On a visiting card, a shutter, a WhatsApp status, an auto's back panel — anyone who scans it lands on his profile and can book him. He has no website; now he does not need one.

The shop

3% from the shop's side — never added to her basket

A kirana's gross margin on staples is 3–6%. An 8% service rate would exceed the entire margin on the items people order most, so retail is priced completely differently: a small commission out of the shop's margin, a delivery fee shown separately, and item prices that are never marked up.

  • 3% of the basket (5% on higher-margin categories)
  • Never less than ₹5, never more than ₹25 (₹50 on those categories)
  • The first 30 orders cost nothing at all
  • No listing fee, no monthly fee, no fee to exist

A shopkeeper can also see her own orders and the payment claimed against each — which sounds obvious, and was broken until the database learned that shops have owners.

The delivery partner

The ride is a distance band, and it is never scaled down

A rider is paid for the distance he travelled, not for the size of the basket he carried. SAAHAA takes a flat ₹5 dispatch from the delivery fee and the rider takes the rest. There is no surge, no rating multiplier, and no formula he cannot check in his head.

“Free delivery” never means unpaid labour. When a shop advertises free delivery over a threshold, the shop absorbs the ride. The rider is paid the full band either way. A promise made to a customer is not allowed to be funded out of the person carrying the bag.

The rail

Two people confirm a payment before it becomes money

There is no payment gateway licence yet, and pretending otherwise would mean holding customers' money on a promise. So the live rail is manual UPI, and it is deliberately slow at exactly one point: the point where a claim turns into a balance.

01
She transfers to one UPI id The screen names saahaa@ptyes and a reference, and says plainly that nobody from SAAHAA will ever ask her to pay a different id. no money has moved inside SAAHAA
02
She types the 12-digit UTR One UTR can be live against one order only — enforced by a partial unique index in Postgres, not by a check that two requests could both pass. still nothing; a claim may never post a ledger leg
03
The pro confirms he can see it He is standing in her doorway and she has her bank app open. His check takes two seconds and lets the job start. the job starts — still no money
04
An admin matches it to the bank statement The only step that funds escrow, and the only one that writes to the ledger. A short payment funds what the statement showed, and records how short. escrow funded · the pro is payable next morning

The risk this accepts, stated plainly: a pro starts work on a claim and is settled next morning on a fact. The gap is bounded to one job, and it is the reason the payout batch runs after the statement is read.

What is unusual here

Four decisions that were not obvious

One picture, forty shops

'Aashirvaad Atta 5kg' is the same product in every kirana, so it resolves to one shared image however many shops stock it. Measured against the real 199-item catalogue: 7,960 listings collapse to 199 pictures — 7.8 MB instead of 311 MB, and the saving grows with every shop that joins.

The console is not in the app

The owner console used to be a route anyone could type. It is now a separate page; in the customer app that address resolves to nothing. No customer is ever told to open it, and no screen mentions it — a plumber was once shown a broken-ledger alarm and sent to Finance.

Fifteen gates before anything ships

306 unit tests, 30 rendered screens, 17 money journeys, the payment rail, the Worker, picture sharing, the QR codes, the admin door, the schema against a real Postgres, 600 randomised order lives, and two scores that cannot be lowered to make a change pass.

Built to cost nothing at zero

Static pages on Cloudflare, a Worker for the one thing a browser must not be trusted with, Postgres on a free tier, images in the repository. The bill at zero orders is zero, which is what lets the first thirty orders per shop be free.

What is not proven yet

Everything above is in the code and covered by a gate. These are not.

Unscanned
The QR codes decode correctly under a reader written to the specification, and a printed one has never been scanned by a real phone. Test one before ordering cards.
Unwired
The database schema runs against a real Postgres in the gates, but Supabase's own auth wiring is proven only when the project is actually created.
Unopened
The owner console needs Cloudflare Access in front of it. The password is a hash and the browser cannot move money — but the door is still on a public street until that is done.
Unused
No customer, pro, shop or rider has used any of this. Every number here is a rule the software enforces, not a result it has produced.
