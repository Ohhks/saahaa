What I understood

Every small shop and independent pro has to hand a large slice of each order to an aggregator, or pay a developer and a yearly plan to be online at all. SAAHAA puts them online for free and charges only when an order lands — roughly a seventh of what running a conventional storefront costs. One circle: customers, service pros, and neighbourhood shops.

Assumptions I made

  • Mobile-first product. Every app screen here is drawn at 352×740 — a real phone canvas, 44px minimum touch targets.
  • Two supply sides in one app, one shell: Pro (quotes jobs, e.g. an electrician) and Shop (sells stock, e.g. a hardware store). Customers see both in one feed.
  • International audience, so copy is plain English, currency shown as a neutral symbol, and no market-specific payment rails are assumed.
  • Copy is written by me and plausible — replace it with your real strings before this goes anywhere near build.

Design context

Built on the Modernist system bound to this project: Archivo throughout, one red accent on a light ground, zero corner radius, 2px rules doing the organising. Note the tension with your logo, which is deep purple and gold — that's a luxury register, this is a utilitarian one. I've kept the system, because it's the attached one, and because a flat high-contrast interface is the right call for shopkeepers on cheap phones in bad light. Say the word and I'll explore a purple/gold variant.

The live site renders through JavaScript, and the codebase folder you attached isn't reachable from my side, so these screens are drawn from your brief rather than from your existing UI. If you can paste screenshots of the current app I'll match its structure.
